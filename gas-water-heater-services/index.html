A gas water heater is a fairly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. When something goes wrong, it is usually at one of those points. In Chattanooga, TN, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Every gas water heater call starts with safety and then moves to testing. If there is a gas odor, leave the home first, call the gas utility from outside, and do not operate switches or try to relight anything. Once the area is safe, our Chattanooga technicians work through the heater systematically: millivolt readings on the thermocouple, a close look at the pilot and main burner flames, gas pressure at the control valve, a draft test to make sure exhaust is not spilling back, and a CO check around the appliance. Scorch marks at the burner access, rust flakes on the burner, and a lazy yellow flame each point to a different problem, and we chase down the right one.

Most gas water heater repairs are finished in a single visit. We replace thermocouples, flame sensors, pilot assemblies, and igniters, clean burners and flame arrestor screens, swap failed gas control valves, and correct vent connectors that have separated or lost their slope. We also flush sediment, the cause of that popping or rumbling sound and a major reason tanks overheat at the bottom and fail early. When the tank itself is leaking or the heater is deep into its service life, we tell you honestly and walk you through replacement. New installations are sized by first-hour rating, not just gallons, and include the right venting, a sediment trap on the gas line, an expansion tank where needed, and seismic straps and a drain pan where code requires them. Warning Signs to Watch For

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • A pilot that lights, then goes out when you release the knob
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • Hot water that runs out faster than it used to
  • Signs of backdrafting, such as soot, condensation, or an exhaust smell at the vent

Why Professional Gas Water Heater Services Matters

Start with the hazards. Fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust laced with carbon monoxide come with every gas water heater. A loose flare or threaded joint can leak gas, a vent that is disconnected or poorly sloped can spill exhaust back into the room, and a gas valve installed without a leak test is a risk you cannot see. Then there is the diagnostic side: a pilot that goes out can mean a weak thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, a clogged flame arrestor, a draft problem, or a failing gas valve, and working through them by guesswork is slow and costly.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Keep the area around the heater clear, especially of gasoline, paint, and solvents
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
