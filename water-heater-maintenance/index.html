The inside of a neglected water heater tells the whole story. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, built to corrode in place of the tank, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. Across Chattanooga, TN, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

Here is what the visit covers. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless systems are included. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that restrict flow and reduce efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and look at the error history for patterns. Whatever type you have, you receive a plain summary of the unit's condition and any parts approaching the end of their life. To schedule a visit in Chattanooga, call (423) 275-2420.

Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • Hot water with a sulfur odor

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Maintenance?

Some upkeep is fair game for homeowners: watching for leaks, keeping the area around the heater clear, and noting new noises. Beyond that, the work gets riskier. Draining a tank means handling scalding water and a drain valve that may not close again if it is old and full of sediment. Pulling an anode rod often takes a breaker bar and real leverage on a fitting that has not moved in years. Testing a T&P valve on an older heater can leave it dripping afterward. On gas units, the burner, thermocouple, and venting involve combustion safety, and a vent that is not drafting can put carbon monoxide into the house.

Our trucks are stocked for the usual surprises: a drain valve that will not reseal, an anode rod that has turned to wire, a relief valve that drips after testing. We carry replacements, along with meters for element and thermostat testing and a descaling kit for tankless units. More than parts, we bring experience. Small signs, like rust at a connection, a weak flame, or a waterlogged expansion tank, tell us where a heater is headed, and catching them early is the entire point of the visit. That is how a routine visit adds years to a heater's life.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
