A tankless water heater keeps no reserve of hot water. It waits for a faucet to open, senses the flow, fires a burner or energizes its elements, and heats water as it races through a compact heat exchanger. That on-demand design is a big part of its appeal, but it packs a lot of sensors and controls into one small cabinet, and every one of them has to agree before you get hot water. When one of them objects, the unit shuts down and posts an error code. In Chattanooga, TN, the usual culprits are mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, a flow sensor that no longer registers demand, an igniter or flame rod that cannot prove the flame, and venting or gas supply problems that trip the unit's safety limits.

Chattanooga Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in Chattanooga, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat gives itself away in those numbers. A code tells us where to look, not what to replace. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

After the fault is pinned down, most repairs are finished in a single visit. We descale heat exchangers with a pump and descaling solution circulated through the service valves, replace worn flow sensors, igniters, flame rods, thermistors, and combustion fans, clean inlet filter screens, and correct venting problems that trip pressure switches. When the real issue is a gas line sized for the old tank heater, we tell you plainly and quote the correct pipe run, because no amount of part swapping will fix a unit that is starved for fuel. When to Call for Tankless Water Heater Repair

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A cold-water sandwich that has gotten noticeably worse
  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • Unusual noises from the cabinet, like a whine, a roar, or a rattle
  • Repeated ignition attempts, then a shutdown
  • Lukewarm water whenever two fixtures run at the same time

Don't Wait for a Total Failure

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. If you notice any of these signs, contact Chattanooga Water Heater at (423) 275-2420 for a prompt inspection.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Consider the risks first. A gas-fired tankless heater can burn nearly 200,000 BTU per hour at full fire, and its vent is the only thing carrying carbon monoxide out of the house; a joint that pulls loose or an intake that gets blocked changes that in a hurry. On the electric side, the loads are large, frequently more than 100 amps spread over several 240-volt circuits. Then there is the diagnostic puzzle. Error codes point to a system, not a part, so a flow sensor fault, a scaled heat exchanger, and a bad thermistor can look nearly identical on the display, and guessing gets expensive.

What you get from a professional is certainty. Instead of swapping parts, our technicians confirm the fault with a meter, a manometer, and temperature readings, so the fix is aimed at the actual cause. Descaling is done with a pump and service valves, not by pouring vinegar into an inlet and hoping. Gas pressure is checked with the burner at full output, vent runs are inspected against the manufacturer's length and clearance limits, and the unit is tested at low and high flow before we leave. Proper service also helps you stay within the manufacturer's warranty terms, many of which expect qualified service and regular descaling.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here are our recommendations:

  • Keep the unit powered during freezing weather so its freeze protection can work
  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year so the flow sensor gets full flow
  • Tell us before adding a gas appliance, so the gas line can be checked for the extra load
  • Protect the heat exchanger with a softener or scale inhibitor when your water is hard
  • Treat a code that returns after a reset as a symptom worth diagnosing, not ignoring
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
