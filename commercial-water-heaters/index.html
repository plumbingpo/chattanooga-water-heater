How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Visible corrosion or water pooling near the tank
  • Loud sediment noise from equipment that runs all day
  • Trouble reaching sanitation temperatures in the kitchen
  • A recirculation loop that no longer seems to do its job
  • Energy costs climbing while usage stays flat
  • The tank giving up in the middle of a rush
  • Scalding bursts or wild temperature swings at showers or sinks
  • A heater old enough that a failure feels like a matter of time

Why Hire a Professional for Commercial Water Heaters?

The scale of commercial equipment changes the job. Gas input is often several times that of a home unit, which affects gas line sizing, meter capacity, combustion air, and venting. Electric models can draw enough current to require serious service capacity. Storage often runs at 140 degrees or higher, so thermostatic mixing valves are needed to deliver safe water at fixtures, and those valves must be matched to the flow and tested. Recirculation loops need the right pump, working check valves, and balancing, or hot water short-circuits back to the heater and distant fixtures stay cold.

Beyond the equipment, there is compliance and timing. Permits and inspections are standard for commercial work, larger tanks may carry extra requirements, and any delay at inspection can keep a kitchen or laundromat closed. Chattanooga Water Heater handles that coordination up front, plans installations for early mornings, evenings, or closed days, and replaces banked units one at a time whenever the layout allows. The result is a system built for your busiest hour, installed correctly, and brought online with as little disruption to your customers as possible.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Verify delivery temperatures at sinks and showers a few times a year
  • Leave space around commercial heaters for airflow and safe servicing
  • Set a flushing schedule based on usage; busy tanks collect sediment fast
  • Confirm the recirculation loop still delivers hot water quickly to the far fixtures
  • Track temperatures at the dish machine and hand sinks as part of routine checks
  • Inspect anode rods regularly, since heavy use consumes them faster
  • Budget for replacement as equipment nears the end of its expected life
